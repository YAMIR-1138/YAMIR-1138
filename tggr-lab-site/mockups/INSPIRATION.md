# Lab and institute sites worth borrowing from

Found by web search (Oct–Nov 2026). The hosts were blocked from this environment, so nothing was seen live: [V] = the technique is described in a search snippet (Awwwards, studio case study, press), [I] = inferred.

| Site | Kind | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| [MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu) | institute, Pentagram | [V] One 7×7 grid makes the logo and a separate glyph for each of 23 groups. Projects cross-link people, groups and papers. |
| [Isomorphic Labs](https://www.isomorphiclabs.com) | company, Pentagram | [V] Animated logo symbol; brand imagery from a custom real-time 3D tool. |
| [Allen Institute](https://alleninstitute.org/brand) | institute, Neville Brody | [V] Grid and type rules first, logo second; magenta, teal and neon instead of bioscience blue. |

| [HHMI Beautiful Biology](https://www.hhmi.org/beautifulbiology) | Webby 2025 | [V] Image-led scrollytelling with short captions. |
| [EPFL+ECAL Lab](https://epfl-ecal-lab.ch) | design lab, Awwwards HM | [V] Explore by technology or topic; a colour per section; ongoing projects marked. |
| [Bright Biotech](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/bright-biotech) | company, Awwwards SOTD | [V] Scroll-driven story through levels of the science; team as a scrolling story. |

Structure only: [Crick](https://numiko.com/projects/the-francis-crick-institute) gives each lab a microsite inside the institute brand; the [Broad](https://thisisvisceral.com/insights/10-research-institute-websites-turning-complexity-into-clarity/) homepage reads like a science publication.

## Ideas ranked for this page

1. Logo as a motion system: a glyph per research area from the logo's geometry, reused as section markers.
2. Scroll-driven zoom: the hero helix goes genome → chromosome → gene → variant as you scroll.
3. Publications as a network: papers as nodes linked by shared genes or authors, hover for the title.
4. Layer toggles on the hero helix that highlight loci.
5. People tiles that open to the person's current project and two papers.
6. Accent shifts by section; a pulsing dot marks active or recruiting studies.
