Translational Genetics and Genomics Research

TGGRA medical genetics lab inside a hospital, and the code that comes out of it.

We look for the genes behind rare disease in the families who come to the Genetics Institute at Tel Aviv Sourasky Medical Center, then work out what those genes do. Along the way we build tools, and we put them here.

Tools

Three things the lab needed and could not buy. All of them are open source and in use on our own bench.

  • Pellaeon

    ChimeraX plugin · Python

    Talk to UCSF ChimeraX in plain English. A chat panel inside ChimeraX turns what you type into commands, shows every command it ran, and asks before anything destructive. Open a structure, compare conformations, map ClinVar variants or conservation onto it, and save a figure with the script that made it.

    • on the ChimeraX Toolshed
    • ChimeraX 1.9 or newer
    • free, paid or local models
    • keys stay on your machine
    • MIT

    Named after the captain of the Chimaera. Not affiliated with UCSF.

    Site and tutorial →Source →Toolshed →

  • GPCompaRe

    Database and reports · structural bioinformatics

    What changes when a receptor switches on. Residue to residue contacts in the inactive and active states of human Class A G protein-coupled receptors, compared receptor by receptor, with a report page for each one and summary statistics across the family.

    • 283 human Class A GPCRs
    • active and inactive conformations
    • contact gained, lost or kept
    • MIT

    Browse receptors →Source →

  • Incubator CO₂ monitor

    Hardware · ESP32 · C++

    A CO₂ and temperature monitor for a cell culture incubator that you build yourself from three parts and six wires. It reads 0 to 10 % CO₂, where ordinary meters stop at 0.5 %, shows both readings on a touchscreen, logs to an SD card and serves a page over Wi-Fi. The guide has the parts list, the wiring, the calibration and ten days of real data.

    • ESP32-2432S028R board
    • Winsen MH-Z16 sensor
    • DS18B20 probe
    • MIT firmware, CC BY 4.0 guide

    A home-built instrument, not a certified one. Calibrate it before you rely on it.

Research

From a family in clinic to a gene, and from the gene back to the family.

The lab discovers new disease-causing genes and studies how they cause disease. The starting point is always a family: people referred to the Genetics Institute with a rare, often inherited condition that existing tests could not explain.

Sequencing and bioinformatics find the candidate. Functional work, tailored to each gene, pathway and phenotype, shows what the variant does. The result is a diagnosis for the family, a mechanism for the field, and sometimes a target for treatment.

  1. STEP 1

    Families

    Referred to the Genetics Institute at Ichilov, with consent to take their question into the lab. Many are consanguineous, which makes recessive disease easier to map.

  2. STEP 2

    Sequencing

    Exome and genome sequencing run in house, with the bioinformatic analysis to find the variants that segregate with disease and rule out the ones that do not.

  3. STEP 3

    Function

    Cell culture and animal models, protein structure, and assays designed for the gene in question, to show that the variant breaks something and how.

  4. STEP 4

    Back to clinic

    A molecular diagnosis, genetic counselling for the family, a new gene in the literature, and where the mechanism allows it, a candidate for personalised therapy.

  • rare monogenic disease
  • novel gene discovery
  • exome and genome sequencing
  • functional genomics
  • GPCR activation and structure
  • clinical genomics
  • lab instruments we build ourselves
