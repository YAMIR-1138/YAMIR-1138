Translational Genetics and Genomics Research Laboratory

The lab studies rare, mostly monogenic disease in families referred to the Genetics Institute. It finds the causative gene by exome and genome sequencing, then works out what the gene does.

This page lists the software and hardware the lab has released. Head of the lab: Prof. Hagit Baris Feldman.

Incubator CO₂ monitor

Calibrate first

A CO₂ and temperature monitor for a cell culture incubator.

You build it yourself from three parts and six wires. It reads 0 to 10 % CO₂, where ordinary meters stop at 0.5 %, shows both readings on a touchscreen, logs to an SD card and serves a page over Wi-Fi. The guide has the parts list, the wiring, the calibration and ten days of real data.

  • ESP32-2432S028R board
  • Winsen MH-Z16 sensor
  • DS18B20 probe

How a project runs

The lab identifies new disease-causing genes and studies how they cause disease. A project usually follows one family from the clinic and back.

  1. Families

    Referred to the Genetics Institute with a rare, often inherited condition that standard testing did not explain. Many are consanguineous, which makes recessive disease easier to map.

  2. Sequencing

    Exome and genome sequencing in house, and the analysis that finds the variants segregating with disease and rules out the ones that do not.

  3. Function

    Cell culture and animal models, protein structure, and assays designed for the gene in question, to show that the variant breaks something and how.

  4. Back to clinic

    A molecular diagnosis and counselling for the family, a new gene in the literature, and where the mechanism allows it, a candidate for treatment.

  • rare monogenic disease
  • novel gene discovery
  • exome and genome sequencing
  • functional genomics
  • GPCR activation and structure
  • clinical genomics
  • lab instruments
